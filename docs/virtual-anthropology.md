# Virtual anthropology

Virtual anthropology applies anthropological questions and methods to social life in digital environments. It can examine how people form relationships, develop norms, assign meaning, and adapt their practices through platforms, online communities, games, and interactions with AI.

## Study practices in context

Attend to participants' perspectives and the conditions shaping their behavior: platform design, moderation, access, community history, and the presence of researchers or automated systems. Interpret observations carefully rather than treating online activity as transparent evidence of a person's identity or intent.

Research should respect privacy, consent, safety, and the expectations of the communities involved. Public availability does not by itself make data ethically uncomplicated. Be clear about methods, limitations, and how observations support conclusions.

## Case note: interpretation, left blank

The section above asks that observations be interpreted carefully rather than read as transparent evidence of identity or intent, that the presence of researchers be counted among the conditions shaping what is observed, and that methods, limitations and the way observations support conclusions be stated plainly. [virtual-anthropology](https://github.com/dermot-r-cochran/virtual-anthropology), the repository the [evidence page](evidence.md) lists for this topic, was created on 1 October 2026 from a prompt its author wrote and a coding agent carried out (pull request #1), and holds its one civilisation, the Archipelago, to a stricter version of that discipline: the platform may compute, restate and trace, and may not interpret at all.

**What was tried.** Every section of a published report carries an epistemic category, observation, metric, hypothesis, method or provenance, interpretation or limitation. The pipeline generates the observations, metrics, method, provenance and limitations, takes the hypotheses as the researcher wrote them, and generates no interpretation: a finding is template text over a declared comparison, `metric comparator threshold`, with one of three outcomes, and holds only "in this single deterministic run"; a chronicle statement is built only from event payloads and snapshot names and cites the event's sequence and hash; and the Interpretation section of every experiment report reads "_Not generated._ The system does not produce interpretations or conclusions beyond the computed findings above. Researchers may add interpretation here." The researcher is in the record as an actor and nowhere else: only identifiers registered at genesis may act, interventions are budgeted by the manifest, and the observer's three questions to the three claimants of *The First Fork* are events 56 to 58 of the log, each answered with the same close, "I cannot settle which interpretation of identity is correct." Two decisions of the author's, both of 1 October, gave the discipline its shape. The first was a required `category` on every manifest, `demonstration` or `experiment`, with no default, the pull request's reason being that "a default would file a run silently" (pull request #3), after his question whether determinism improves or undermines the results had been answered that the platform's is a gain and the scenario's a limitation; a demonstration's report prints the caveat verbatim, that a consistent finding shows the platform and the declared law tables produce the declared outcome and "is not evidence about what citizens would do". The second removed randomness altogether, in his words "Use branching timelines instead of randomness" (pull request #7): wherever a mind has more than one option it records a choice point and takes the first, and a study explores the alternatives by branching in a declared order rather than sampling by seed, so no run is a sample of anything. Since 23 October (pull requests #18 and #19) a citizen may be a plural system, one person in the record with members who front, co-front and differ, and the first of the questions below, whose practices are visible, has a worked answer: a membership of 720,000 is recorded without a record per member, members' disagreement is reconciled by the system's own declared way and written down as ordinary difference, and the vocabulary is the plural community's own.

**What the record says happened.** Each of those has a test the repository's READMEs name, under the README-proof convention of the [engineering intelligence](engineering-intelligence.md#case-note-a-readme-that-names-its-proof) page: the blank interpretation and the standard sections (`simulation/test/research.test.ts`: "experiment report has the standard sections and never generates interpretation"), the mechanical findings, the traced chronicle ("every chronicle statement traces to real events with matching hashes"), the withheld verdict ("three claimants claim continuity; evidence returns no verdict"), the category refused when missing, and for the plural citizen that it is one record, one vote and no copy, and that the scenario report, timeline, chronicle, dataset and manifest use no clinical word (`simulation/test/two-voices.test.ts`). The suites stand at 51 vitest tests in six files, five of them fast-check properties, and 12 unittest tests for the Evennia bridge, and since 31 October and 4 November the repository's own doc check holds every cited test and every count in its prose to the suites, 57 citations and 12 counts by its own statement. The published record is two demonstrations, committed as exports and regenerated and diffed byte for byte on every pull request: *The First Fork* (92 events, 7 ticks, 11 citizen records, three hypotheses consistent) and *Two Voices in One Record* (34 events, 5 ticks, 4 citizen records, four hypotheses consistent). The First Fork's study is where the discipline shows. Twelve branches, each one choice taken differently, all consistent; five timelines, the control reproducing the base head hash; and four doctrine variants, of which one, Fork's descendants receiving no citizenship, ran to 91 events and came out inconsistent with the third hypothesis (no vote was refused outside an electorate in that run), and one, a single descendant per fork, is tabled as "infeasible: The First Fork: fork did not occur". Both stand in the table beside the others; the infeasible one is, in the methodology's words, a result, not a crash. Two things the record does not support. No experiment has been filed: both published runs are demonstrations, whose consistent findings, by the repository's own caveat, show what the platform and the law tables produce and not what citizens would do, and the protagonists are scripted and the other minds are rule bases the scenario declares, so the practices observed are the author's rules, which the limitations page says plainly ("results describe the rules, not emergent cognition"). And the one section that would carry an anthropological reading is empty in both exports. The researcher-commentary template published with *The First Fork*, with its sections for weighted evidence, defensible interpretations and reflexivity ("How might your conversations or interventions have shaped the record?"), has never been filled, and no issue in the repository records a plan to fill it. The discipline of not generating interpretation is proved; the discipline of writing one is untried.

**What changed as a result.** The last of the questions below, what the evidence can support and where interpretation should remain tentative, has in this repository an answer by construction: the evidence supports the comparisons declared before the run and nothing wider, and interpretation is not tentative but absent, left to a named researcher under a label. The questions about consent, privacy and the effect of publication on participants have no human participant there to bear on, and the repository says as much of its plural citizen, which "is not a theory of plurality, and nothing in the data bears on any real system or person". What the record has not yet shown is the platform used for the inquiry it was built for: a run filed as an experiment, whose outcome is not scripted, or an interpretation written against an existing export and kept apart from the observations as the template asks. The repository's own list of gaps names the next step on the research side, "a declared comparison between groups with its own operationalisation, never a generated one", and *Two Voices* names its own, the same season under the other three reconciliations as a study. Either is work for that repository, and its record would belong here. Everything above describes the repository as it stood on 8 November 2026.

## Questions to ask

- Whose practices are visible, and whose are missing?
- How do platform features and community norms shape behavior?
- What consent and privacy expectations apply?
- How might observation or publication affect participants?
- What can the evidence support, and where should interpretation remain tentative?
